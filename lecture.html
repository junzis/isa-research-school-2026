Leveraging Open Data and Models to Reduce Aviation’s Climate Footprint

ISA Research School · May 2026

Overview

  1. Open flight data: turn surveillance data into cleaned trajectories for climate analysis.
  2. Emissions: use aircraft performance models to estimate fuel burn and pollutants.
  3. Weather and contrails: connect trajectories to meteorology and persistent-contrail conditions.
  4. Optimization: compare fuel-optimal, contrail-aware, and operationally feasible trajectories.

Objective: understand how open aviation data, performance models, weather data, and optimization can be combined into a reproducible workflow for reducing aviation climate impact.

The question that started this work

Is it possible to use only open data to study aircraft performance, simulate trajectories, infer fuel burn, and estimate emissions?

  • This was the core research question behind OpenAP.
  • The answer became a research stack: OpenSky, pyopensky, OpenAP, FastMeteo, contrail models, and opentop.
  • Today’s lecture is about how that stack becomes a climate-impact workflow.

Why openness matters in climate mitigation

  • Aviation climate studies combine many uncertain assumptions.
  • Proprietary data and closed models make those assumptions hard to inspect.
  • Open workflows let others rerun, audit, challenge, and extend the result.
  • This is especially important when outputs support operational or policy decisions.

Open Flight Data

OpenSky is more than state vectors

  • State vectors: positions, velocities, altitude, vertical rate.
  • Raw Mode S: aircraft-derived parameters, richer surveillance messages.
  • Flight lists: historical flight-level filtering and aggregation.
  • Aircraft database: type and registration lookup for model assignment.
  • ADS-C: remote and oceanic trajectory/intention information.

From Trajectories to Emissions

Why OpenAP exists

  • Surveillance tells us what happened.
  • Performance models estimate the physical effort required.
  • Fuel and emissions need aircraft type, engine, mass, speed, altitude, and atmosphere.
  • OpenAP was built to make that bridge open and reproducible.

OpenAP model stack

Kinematic

Speed, altitude, and vertical-rate envelopes from open trajectories

Dynamic

Drag polar, thrust, fuel flow, aircraft mass sensitivity

Environment

OpenAP emission indices; FastMeteo for wind, temperature, and humidity

Optimization

Trajectory objectives, constraints, and climate-aware routing

Kinematics: learning flight behavior from data

  • Open trajectories reveal common speed and vertical-rate profiles.
  • These models support simulation and trajectory generation.
  • They also expose where the observed trajectory is operationally unusual.

Dynamic models: drag, thrust, and mass

  • Dynamic models connect flight state to drag, thrust, fuel flow, and mass.
  • Drag-polar coefficients can be estimated from open ADS-B trajectories.
  • Mass is the hardest hidden state: it drives climb, fuel burn, and prediction.
  • Particle filters combine ADS-B climb-rate evolution with OpenAP dynamics.
  • Recent work uses the EUROCONTROL PRC Data Challenge 2024 dataset.

Paper: https://doi.org/10.59490/joas.2025.8252

Bottom-up emission estimation

  • Start from individual trajectories, not schedules or great-circle routes.
  • Estimate fuel and emissions for every flight segment.
  • Aggregate by grid cell, airline, FIR, aircraft type, or flight level.
  • More realistic than schedule + great-circle approximations.
  • Captures route, altitude, and holding/inefficiency effects.
  • Limitation: surveillance coverage, mass assumptions, and model accuracy.

AeroSCOPE: using open data to tackle emissions coverage gaps

  • AeroSCOPE combines open traffic sources into a global route and emissions database.
  • It addresses data gaps with estimation methods where regional datasets are missing.
  • Population and econometric proxies can estimate flows in regions with weak surveillance coverage, such as Africa, South America, and parts of Asia.
  • Dataset and code: https://github.com/AeroMAPS/AeroSCOPE_dataset

Contrails and Weather

Contrails are where the open-data workflow becomes fragile

  • CO2 is tied directly to fuel burn.
  • Contrails depend on atmospheric state, altitude, time, and persistence.
  • A small subset of flights can dominate short-term warming.
  • Forecast uncertainty can decide whether a rerouting is beneficial or harmful.

Weather data bottlenecks led to FastMeteo

  • Contrail prediction needs weather sampled along each trajectory.
  • Traditional workflows move large GRIB/NetCDF files to extract small slices.
  • For millions of flights, this becomes the computational bottleneck.
  • FastMeteo interpolates ERA5 and forecast fields at trajectory points.
  • Useful for wind-aware fuel estimation and contrail screening.

What parameters do contrail methods need?

  • Pressure or altitude from the trajectory.
  • Temperature from meteorological data.
  • Humidity from meteorological data.
  • Formation condition: Schmidt-Appleman criterion.
  • Persistence condition: ice supersaturation.

Add real weather with FastMeteo

Use the example flight from the FastMeteo paper workflow. The paper notebook used Grid; the current API uses ArcoEra5.

import pandas as pd

Diagnose contrail regions with OpenAP

from openap import aero, contrail

flight_contrail = flight_weather.assign(
    rhi=lambda d: contrail.relative_humidity(
        d.specific_humidity,
        aero.pressure(d.altitude * aero.ft),
        d.temperature,
        to="ice",
    ),
    crit_temp=lambda d: contrail.critical_temperature_water(
        aero.pressure(d.altitude * aero.ft)
    ),
    sac=lambda d: d.temperature < d.crit_temp,
    issr=lambda d: d.rhi > 1,
    persistent=lambda d: d.sac & d.issr,
)

Altitude changes can help

  • Contrail-diversion analyses suggest large mitigation potential.
  • Around half of persistent contrails can be avoided with altitude changes in a simplified setup.
  • The hard question is whether the idea survives operational constraints.
  • Paper: Contrail minimization through altitude diversions: A feasibility study leveraging global data
    https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trip.2023.100953

Climate-Optimal Routing

What was ignored in simple altitude-diversion studies?

  • Altitude diversion shows mitigation potential, but it is not yet operational routing.
  • A flight-plan change also changes speed, lateral path, wind exposure, fuel burn, and sector demand.
  • This leads to the next feasibility question: can climate-optimal routing work as an ATM procedure?
  • Paper: Contrail, or not contrail, that is the question: the “feasibility” of climate-optimal routing
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13907

This is the point where “contrail mitigation potential” becomes a routing, network, and governance problem.

The climate-optimal routing paper asks four questions

  • Fuel: can we reduce contrails with reasonable excess fuel?
  • Weather: can forecasts support rerouting decisions?
  • Network: are contrail-optimized trajectories sustainable for air-traffic-flow management?
  • Governance: who is responsible for climate-optimal routing?
  • Open workflow: https://github.com/junzis/contrail-or-not

From feasibility questions to 4D trajectory generation

  • To answer the feasibility questions, we need complete trajectories, not altitude tweaks on fixed routes.
  • opentop optimizes altitude, speed, timing, and lateral path together.
  • Wind, aircraft performance, and gridded atmospheric costs enter the same nonlinear optimal-control problem.
  • This creates comparable fuel-optimal and climate-aware trajectories.

Cost grids turn weather into optimization input

  • Contrail-forming regions become gridded costs.
  • The optimizer samples the grid along candidate trajectories.
  • Fuel and contrail terms must be balanced explicitly.
  • Smooth interpolation reduces discontinuities in the optimization problem.
  • ERA5 reanalysis versus ARPEGE forecast on 20 February 2022.

The weather-derived cost grid from the previous slide becomes df_cost. The optimizer then balances gridded contrail cost against CO2 from fuel burn.

interpolant = top.tools.interpolant_from_dataframe(df_cost)

def objective(x, u, dt, **kwargs):
    vtas = openap.casadi.aero.mach2tas(u[0], x[2])
    contrail_cost = optimizer.obj_grid_cost(
        x, u, dt, n_dim=3, time_dependent=False, **kwargs
    ) * vtas * 1e-3
    co2_cost = optimizer.obj_fuel(x, u, dt, **kwargs) * 7.03e-15
    return contrail_cost + co2_cost

Fuel versus contrail trade-off

  • Compare original flight, fuel-optimal flight, and contrail-aware variants.
  • A higher contrail penalty can reduce contrail distance, but the fuel penalty and route deviation must be reported together.
  • Vertical profiles: contrail-aware trajectories often shift altitude to avoid persistent regions.
  • The challenge is whether avoidance is robust, affordable, and operationally acceptable.

Forecast versus reanalysis

  • Operational decisions must use forecast data.
  • Retrospective evaluation often uses reanalysis.
  • If forecast and reanalysis disagree, optimized decisions can be misplaced.
  • This is why contrail-routing studies must separate evaluation data from decision data.

Capacity changes when many aircraft reroute

  • A single contrail-avoidance trajectory may look feasible.
  • Many such trajectories can shift demand into other sectors.
  • Capacity and fairness become part of the climate objective.

Satellite check: what happened that day?

  • Satellite observations provide an external check on model-based predictions.
  • They also reveal the limits of simple formation estimates.
  • Future workflows need data fusion: surveillance, meteorology, satellite, and ground cameras.

Who should be responsible?

  • Airlines control fuel and operating costs.
  • ANSPs and network managers control flow and capacity.
  • Passengers and society experience costs and benefits differently.
  • Weather uncertainty and monitoring affect accountability.
  • OPTIMAL (TU Delft - France Initiative) extends this into shared decision-making and operational feasibility.
  • FairSky (SESAR ER3) addresses fairness when climate-optimal routing changes costs and capacity.

Takeaways

The reusable workflow

  1. Get trajectories: OpenSky and pyopensky.
  2. Estimate fuel and emissions: OpenAP.
  3. Add weather and contrail conditions: FastMeteo and OpenAP contrail tools.
  4. Compare decisions through optimization: use opentop to test fuel, contrail, forecast, capacity, and responsibility trade-offs.
